01 // THE BASICS

What Is Phishing?

Phishing is a form of social engineering in which an attacker pretends to be a trustworthy person, company, or organization to manipulate someone into taking an unsafe action. The attacker may try to steal passwords, financial information, verification codes, or other sensitive data.

The Impersonation

An attacker pretends to be someone you recognize or trust, such as a delivery service, bank, employer, school, or online platform.

The Manipulation

The message may create urgency, fear, curiosity, or excitement to make you act before thinking carefully.

The Objective

The attacker may want your credentials, personal information, money, access to an account, or an opportunity to compromise your device.

  1. Impersonation
  2. Manipulation
  3. Unsafe Action

Not every suspicious-looking message is malicious. Evaluate several clues together rather than relying on a single detail.

02 // ATTACK TYPES

Know Your Enemy

Phishing takes different forms depending on the channel, target, and method of deception. Understanding these patterns makes suspicious messages easier to recognize.

03 // RED FLAGS

Red Flags You Should Never Ignore

Phishing messages are not always obvious. A professional-looking message can still be deceptive, and spelling mistakes alone do not prove that a message is fraudulent.

    Remember: A single clue is not always proof of phishing. Evaluate the full context and verify suspicious requests through a trusted, independently obtained contact method.

      07 // DEFENSE

      Your Defense Starts Here

      You do not need to become a cybersecurity expert to reduce your risk. A few consistent habits can make it harder for attackers to deceive you.

        When in doubt, do not use the message to verify itself. Find a trusted way to contact the organization independently.

        Make Every Click a Conscious Decision.

        Cybersecurity begins with awareness. Learn the signs, question unexpected requests, and help others build safer digital habits.
